Why email security and phishing protection matters in Dallas

Dallas–Fort Worth is one of the largest business regions in the US: corporate headquarters, financial services and insurance, real estate, logistics around DFW Airport and the I-35 and I-20 corridors, and thousands of professional firms and auto dealers. For small and mid-sized businesses here, business email compromise and ransomware are the incidents that actually show up.

For financial services, insurance and auto dealers, the target is customer financial data and wire transfers, and many of these businesses fall under the FTC Safeguards Rule. Wire fraud through business email compromise is the most direct financial risk for these businesses.

For logistics, trucking and distribution the pattern looks different but rhymes: load coordination by email invites payment fraud and double-brokering scams using look-alike domains.

What we see in law firms, accounting firms and professional services

In law firms, accounting firms and professional services, the attacker wants access to many clients at once: tax data, contracts and emails with banking details. The firm is a trusted sender for its clients; if its domain is spoofed, fraud arrives in the firm's name. Tax preparers are also covered by the FTC Safeguards Rule. What is business email compromise (BEC)?

It is when someone sends an email that looks like it comes from you or a vendor, often with a real invoice but different bank details. It works when the domain has no DMARC or when a real mailbox was already taken over. We prevent it by fixing email authentication and watching look-alike domains.

Is the phishing simulation required?

No, it is an add-on. Many businesses start with email configuration and domain monitoring and add simulations when they want to measure their team.
